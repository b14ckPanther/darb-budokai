Three ways to draw the world

All three keep the approved seal brand: night, forest, gold and ivory, ki orange only as light, Zen Antique for headings and Geist for everything you read. They differ in what the map is made of.

A. Seal Stone

The arena floor at night, tile by tile, and light only where you fought today.

Dark tiled stone carved into relief, gold contour lines like an engraved survey, forest panels. Ki orange glows only from cracks where real events landed. Neglect is pale stone dust settling over a region. Chrome is gold hairlines and generous dark space.

Recommended. It is the brand mark grown into a map: the seal struck into stone, cracked with ki.

B. Gold Orrery

A tournament instrument: regions are stone plates turning on gold rails.

The four regions float as tiered plates around a central ring, connected by fine gold rails. The day's encounters orbit slowly. Precise and elegant, but it reads as a device more than a place, and it can drift toward a sci-fi dashboard.

Strong for the skill tree (a constellation of rings), weaker as the everyday map.

C. Ink and Ki

An ink-wash landscape on ivory paper where the weather is your state.

Monochrome forest ink on ivory, one ki-orange sun. Stamina is clear sky, neglect is rain and mist. Calm and beautiful in daylight, but status relies on weather and shape, colour range is narrow, and gold loses its meaning on light paper.
